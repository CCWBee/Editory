Neo-Anglo-Norman

Not the worship of the ashes — the veneration of the flame.

Jersey has its own architectural language — pink granite hewn from the cliffs, lime render, slate-clad roofs, sash windows deep-set in stone, gable ends with chimneys at each shoulder. The thesis of Neo-Anglo-Norman is that this vocabulary deserves to be a living tradition: a flame carried forward, not a heritage costume worn on listed buildings.

Editory is a sandbox for that argument. Pull the levers — preset, material, weathering, light, rain — and watch a harbour front recompose itself. The point is not to design a particular building. The point is to feel out what the language can do when you let it breathe in contemporary materials and proportions without losing the salt air it was made for.

Granite is the start of the conversation, not the end of it. St Aubin's painted Georgian fronts, the granite-and-glass of the new Government building, the cottage cob of the inland parishes — and one day a Jersey-rooted future register with deeper reveals and bigger openings — all of it is in scope. What is out of scope is glass-steel-concrete brutalism with no relation to place.

Pink granite the cliffs of La Corbière, Mont Orgueil
Lime render the warm white of civic buildings
Slate roofs, ridge caps, occasional cladding
Timber + stone a quieter rural register
Fibreglass a contemporary intervention
Weathering lichen, sea-spray, the patina of place

Three mutation presets carry the language sideways and forward — sat alongside the canonical four:

Bow-front Georgian painted-render plot with a shallow seafront bow; the St Aubin's register
Granite + glass granite ground course with vertical granite fins and a glass curtain wall above; the new Government building
Future register granite base, lighter upper, deep stone reveals around bigger openings — the flame carried forward
